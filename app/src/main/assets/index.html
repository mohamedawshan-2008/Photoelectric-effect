What is the photoelectric effect?

When light shines on a metal, it can knock electrons out of the surface. Light carries energy in tiny packets called photons, and each photon gives all of its energy to one electron.

An electron only escapes if one photon has at least a minimum energy, called the work function (W) of the metal. If the light's frequency is too low, nothing is emitted, even with very bright light. Sodium and caesium have small work functions, so even visible light works on them.

Energy of one photon

E = hf    f = c/λ    E = hc/λ

h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s (Planck's constant), c = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s, λ = wavelength. A handy shortcut: E (eV) = 1239.84 ÷ λ (nm). Shorter wavelength means higher energy.

Unit: 1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, the energy of one electron moved through 1 volt.

Kinetic energy and stopping voltage

Kmax = hf − W    Kmax = eVs

The fastest emitted electrons have kinetic energy hf − W. If you make the collector negative, it pushes electrons back. At the stopping voltage Vs, even the fastest electrons are turned around and the current drops to zero. So Vs in volts equals Kmax in eV.

What the graph shows

Positive voltage: all emitted electrons reach the collector, so the current is flat (saturation current).

More intensity: more photons, more electrons, higher saturation current. The stopping voltage stays the same.

Shorter wavelength: more energetic photons, so a larger stopping voltage.

Below the threshold wavelength (λ₀ = 1239.84 ÷ W): no current at all.

Try these

1. Set sodium and 250 nm, then plot the curve. Read the stopping voltage.
2. Lower the intensity and plot again. What changes, and what does not?
3. Increase the wavelength slowly until the current vanishes. Compare this with λ₀.
4. Switch to zinc, then platinum, and repeat. Which wavelengths still work? Platinum has the highest work function here.

Solar power

Solar cells use a related effect in silicon. Light frees electrons, and their flow through a circuit is electric current. One cell gives only about 0.6 V, so many cells are joined to raise the voltage.

Note: this lab uses a simplified model. Work functions are from the Handbook of Chemistry and Physics (via HyperPhysics, Georgia State University). They are for clean metal surfaces and change a little with surface condition and crystal face (for example, silver is 4.26 eV polycrystalline but up to 4.74 eV on some faces).
